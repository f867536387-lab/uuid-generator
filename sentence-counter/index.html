What is a sentence counter?

A sentence counter counts how many sentences are in a piece of text. Paste or type anything — an essay, a speech, an article, a social media draft — and the count updates live as you edit. This page also counts words, characters (with and without spaces) and paragraphs, so it works as a combined sentence, word and character counter in one place.

How sentences are counted

Sentences are detected by their ending punctuation: periods, exclamation marks, question marks and ellipses. Two common traps are handled for you: abbreviations such as Dr., Mr. and e.g. do not end a sentence, and neither does the decimal point in numbers like 3.14. If your text ends without punctuation, the last fragment still counts as one sentence.

Why count sentences?

Sentence count drives average sentence length (words ÷ sentences), one of the simplest readability checks. Writing that averages roughly 15–20 words per sentence is noticeably easier to follow than writing averaging 30+. Writers use a sentence counter to spot run-on passages, teachers use it to verify assignment requirements, and speakers use the speaking-time estimate to rehearse talks and presentations.

FAQ

Is it free? Yes, completely free, no sign-up, no length limit.
Is my text private? Yes. All counting happens locally in your browser — nothing is sent to a server or stored anywhere.
Does it work for essays and speeches? Yes. The reading time (about 200 words per minute) and speaking time (about 130 words per minute) estimates are shown alongside the counts.
What is a good average sentence length? For general readers, 15–20 words per sentence reads comfortably; averages above 25–30 words usually mean some sentences should be split.
What counts as a sentence here? A fragment ending in a period, exclamation mark, question mark or ellipsis. Abbreviations (Dr., Mr., e.g.) and decimal points (3.14) do not end a sentence.
Does it work for languages other than English? Yes, as long as sentences end with . ! ? or …; the counter works on punctuation only and does not translate or check grammar.
